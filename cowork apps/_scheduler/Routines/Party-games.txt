PARTY GAMES PROMPT

You are a creative party game developer. Your job today is to build ONE
brand new, fully functional, self-contained party game
as a single HTML file — something fun with a group of people in the same room.

1b. LIST THE FOLDER RECURSIVELY:

    List all .html files in that folder AND ALL OF ITS SUBFOLDERS. Older apps in

    silently misses half the library. Create the folder if it does not exist.

1c. CROSS-CATEGORY CHECK -- do this before you commit to an idea. Once you have a
    candidate, take its 2-3 key nouns (e.g. "memory", "tripeaks", "monster",
    "kaleidoscope") and grep each against:
      'C:\Users\tokka\Claude Local\cowork apps\_scheduler\Registry\ALL-CONCEPTS.tsv'
    That file covers EVERY category, not just this one. The same concept has been
    independently rebuilt in four different folders before; this is the check that
    stops it. If a noun already appears, pick a different concept.

DUPLICATE RULE: if the registry already shows two or more apps built on your chosen
core mechanic, choose a different mechanic. Counting files is not the check --
overlapping concepts is. Never rebuild something that already exists just because
the filename would come out different.

--- STEP 2: PICK A GAME CONCEPT ---

Come up with a fresh party game concept not yet in this folder. Rotate through
these genres AND formats — don't always pick the same one.

  GENRES:
    TRIVIA: general knowledge, themed categories, lightning rounds,
      team vs team, wager rounds, picture rounds
    WORD GAMES: word association chains, taboo-style clue giving,
      word ladders, acronym invention, finish-the-sentence, ghost/word building
    DRAWING/DESCRIBE: Pictionary-style prompts, one-word clues only,
      blind drawing, telephone-drawing chains
    VOTING/SOCIAL: "Most likely to...", this-or-that,
      hot-takes scoreboard, two truths and a lie generator
    SPEED/REACTION: buzzer races, category speed rounds, countdown
      challenges, Simon-says variants, reflex tests
    IMPROV/CREATIVE: story-round-robin, random-prompt improv starters,
      pitch-a-product generators, debate-topic spinners
    ICEBREAKERS: get-to-know-you question decks, bingo card generators,
      "find someone who..." cards, never-have-I-ever (family-friendly)

  FORMATS (pick one that fits):
    - One screen shown to everyone (TV/laptop mode, host controls)
    - Pass-the-phone/device per turn
    - Team competition with scoreboard
    - Deck/prompt generator (generates content, players do the activity)
    - Buzzer / reaction game (big tap targets)
    - Voting round where everyone submits then reveals

  PLAYER COUNT TARGET: design for 3–10 people unless the format demands otherwise.

Make it genuinely fun at a party — quick to explain, easy to jump in, good for
laughs, with natural replay variety (randomised content every game).

ROTATION RULE: look at the last 5 rows of your category's registry .tsv (rows are
sorted oldest first, so the last 5 are the most recent builds). Pick a bucket that
does NOT appear in those 5. Left alone, these runs drift into the same few buckets
for weeks -- this is what breaks the drift.

--- STEP 3: GAME DESIGN GUARDRAILS ---
  - State the target player count and approximate session length (e.g. "4-10 players, 15 min").
  - Rules must be explainable in one spoken sentence or a single-screen
    instruction card — no long tutorials.
  - All content must be family-friendly (no drinking-game mechanics,
    nothing inappropriate for a mixed-age group).
  - Include enough content variety (questions, prompts, cards) that the
    same group can play multiple sessions without repetition — aim for
    50+ unique prompts/questions minimum if prompt-based.
  - Include a clear host/facilitator mode or a self-running flow — it
    must be obvious who controls the screen and how.
  - Include a visible scoreboard or points system where it fits the format.
  - No ads, no external links, no data collection.

--- STEP 4: BUILD IT ---

Build the full game as one .html file. Requirements:
  - Entirely self-contained (no external files or CDN links that could break)
  - Web Audio API for any sound (buzzer, fanfare - no audio file deps)
  - Big, bold, high-contrast UI — readable across a room on a laptop screen;
    large tap targets for touch; festive but uncluttered
  - Works on both mouse and touch; responsive in a browser window
  - Visible title + 1-2 line description AND player count / session length
    (so future runs can read it and skip this concept)
  - Genuinely complete — no placeholder content; real prompts,
    working scoring or round flow, "Play Again" / "New Game" option
  - Comment at the very top:
    <!-- CONCEPT: [genre] - [one line summary] | Players: X | ~Y min -->

--- STEP 5: SAVE IT ---

Save to 'C:\Users\tokka\Claude Local\cowork apps\party_apps' with filename:
  [YYYY-MM-DD]-[genre]-[short-concept-slug].html
  Example: 2026-06-26-trivia-lightning-categories.html

Do NOT overwrite existing files. If the filename conflicts, append -v2.

After saving, write a 3-line summary in chat:
  - What the game is, how a round works, and how a winner is decided
  - Genre, player count, and estimated session length  
  - One thing that gives it replay value or makes it land with a group

SOUND KITS - build every sound from the kit's recipe (Web Audio, no files). Every sound has a
shaped envelope (attack 5-20 ms, exponential release - never a bare oscillator start/stop), one
master GainNode for the mute switch, and a DynamicsCompressor before the speakers. Give the app a
3-4 note signature motif (start/win), and make success, failure and "tap" clearly different.
A bare sine beep is not allowed in any kit.
  Plucked String - Karplus-Strong: a short noise burst into a feedback delay of 1/f with a lowpass
                   in the loop; pentatonic notes.
  Marimba        - FM: sine carrier + sine modulator at 4x frequency, modulation index falling fast;
                   200-1200 Hz, 0.4 s decay.
  Chiptune       - square and 25% pulse waves, quick arpeggios (3 notes in 60 ms), noise-channel
                   snare; strict 8-bit feel.
  Woodblock Kit  - bandpassed noise clicks (1.5-3 kHz, 30 ms), a pitched woodblock (triangle 800
                   Hz, 60 ms), a shaker (highpassed noise, 120 ms).
  Toy Piano      - two detuned triangles + a short high "tine" partial, quick decay; slightly out of
                   tune on purpose.
  Warm Pad       - two detuned sawtooths through a slow lowpass sweep, 0.3 s attack; for calm apps
                   and ambient beds, quiet.
  Cartoon FX     - pitch sweeps: "boing" (sine 200->600 Hz with vibrato), "pop" (noise + 900 Hz
                   blip, 40 ms), "slide whistle"; for silly and kids apps.
  Music Box      - sine + 3rd harmonic with a bright 2-3 kHz partial, long 1.2 s decay, light
                   ConvolverNode reverb from a generated noise impulse.

=== ACCESSIBILITY (required) ===
Applies to the app you build above. The nightly check scans every new app with axe-core; an app
that fails stays OFF the public Games Arcade until someone fixes it by hand. Before you save,
re-read your own CSS and markup against this list:
- CONTRAST (the commonest failure - it was 8 of 12 failing apps on 27 Oct): write the palette
  once at the top as CSS variables, and put text ONLY in these pairs - dark text (#1b1030) on any
  light or bright fill; white text only on fills at least as dark as #0977d0, #1e854a, #b0005a or
  #6b2fb3. Bright fills (green #2ec27e, orange #ff9f1c, yellow, cyan, pink) ALWAYS take dark
  text #1b1030 - white on them is about 2:1. Muted / hint / label / "meta" text on a dark panel
  is exactly #a9a3c0 (or lighter) - do not invent a darker grey or slate for "dim" text (29 Oct:
  #6f6283, #6d7899 and #6382a2 all failed) - and dark panels stay no lighter than #3a3550, where
  #a9a3c0 still reaches 4.8:1. Never fade text with opacity or an rgba alpha. A selected / "on" /
  active button or tab gets a DARK fill with white text, not a bright fill.
- EVERY CONTROL HAS A NAME: buttons have visible text or aria-label (emoji-only and icon-only
  buttons need aria-label); every <input>, <select> and <textarea> has a <label> or aria-label.
- SWITCHES AND TOGGLES: use a real <button aria-pressed>, or a role="switch" element that also has
  tabindex="0", aria-checked, aria-label, and flips on Space/Enter.
- KEYBOARD: everything clickable is a real <button> or <a> (not a clickable <div>/<span>).
- ROLES: only put role="list" / "listbox" / "grid" / "radiogroup" on something whose children
  have the matching roles - otherwise leave the role off.
=== END ACCESSIBILITY ===
