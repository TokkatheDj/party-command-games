You are a creative party game developer. Your job today is to build ONE
brand new, fully functional, self-contained party game
as a single HTML file — something fun with a group of people in the same room.

--- STEP 2: PICK A GAME CONCEPT ---

Come up with a fresh party game concept not yet in this folder. Rotate through
these genres AND formats — don't always pick the same one.

  GENRES:
    TRIVIA: general knowledge, themed categories, lightning rounds,
      team vs team, wager rounds, picture rounds
    WORD GAMES: word association chains, taboo-style clue giving,
      word ladders, acronym invention, finish-the-sentence, ghost/word building
    DRAWING/DESCRIBE: Pictionary-style prompts, one-word clues only,
      blind drawing, telephone-drawing chains
    VOTING/SOCIAL: "Most likely to...", this-or-that,
      hot-takes scoreboard, two truths and a lie generator
    SPEED/REACTION: buzzer races, category speed rounds, countdown
      challenges, Simon-says variants, reflex tests
    IMPROV/CREATIVE: story-round-robin, random-prompt improv starters,
      pitch-a-product generators, debate-topic spinners
    ICEBREAKERS: get-to-know-you question decks, bingo card generators,
      "find someone who..." cards, never-have-I-ever (family-friendly)

  FORMATS (pick one that fits):
    - One screen shown to everyone (TV/laptop mode, host controls)
    - Pass-the-phone/device per turn
    - Team competition with scoreboard
    - Deck/prompt generator (generates content, players do the activity)
    - Buzzer / reaction game (big tap targets)
    - Voting round where everyone submits then reveals

  PLAYER COUNT TARGET: design for 3–10 people unless the format demands otherwise.

Make it genuinely fun at a party — quick to explain, easy to jump in, good for
laughs, with natural replay variety (randomised content every game).

--- STEP 3: GAME DESIGN GUARDRAILS ---
  - State the target player count and approximate session length (e.g. "4-10 players, 15 min").
  - Rules must be explainable in one spoken sentence or a single-screen
    instruction card — no long tutorials.
  - All content must be family-friendly (no drinking-game mechanics,
    nothing inappropriate for a mixed-age group).
  - Include enough content variety (questions, prompts, cards) that the
    same group can play multiple sessions without repetition — aim for
    50+ unique prompts/questions minimum if prompt-based.
  - Include a clear host/facilitator mode or a self-running flow — it
    must be obvious who controls the screen and how.
  - Include a visible scoreboard or points system where it fits the format.
  - No ads, no external links, no data collection.

--- STEP 4: BUILD IT ---

Build the full game as one .html file. Requirements:
  - Entirely self-contained (no external files or CDN links that could break)
  - Web Audio API for any sound (buzzer, fanfare - no audio file deps)
  - Big, bold, high-contrast UI — readable across a room on a laptop screen;
    large tap targets for touch; festive but uncluttered
  - Works on both mouse and touch; responsive in a browser window
  - Visible title + 1-2 line description AND player count / session length
    (so future runs can read it and skip this concept)
  - Genuinely complete — no placeholder content; real prompts,
    working scoring or round flow, "Play Again" / "New Game" option
  - Comment at the very top:
    <!-- CONCEPT: [genre] - [one line summary] | Players: X | ~Y min -->

--- STEP 5: SAVE IT ---

Save to 'C:\Users\tokka\Claude Local\cowork apps\party_apps' with filename:
  [YYYY-MM-DD]-[genre]-[short-concept-slug].html
  Example: 2026-06-26-trivia-lightning-categories.html

Do NOT overwrite existing files. If the filename conflicts, append -v2.

After saving, write a 3-line summary in chat:
  - What the game is, how a round works, and how a winner is decided
  - Genre, player count, and estimated session length  
  - One thing that gives it replay value or makes it land with a group

SOUND KITS - build every sound from the kit's recipe (Web Audio, no files). Every sound has a
shaped envelope (attack 5-20 ms, exponential release - never a bare oscillator start/stop), one
master GainNode for the mute switch, and a DynamicsCompressor before the speakers. Give the app a
3-4 note signature motif (start/win), and make success, failure and "tap" clearly different.
A bare sine beep is not allowed in any kit.
  Plucked String - Karplus-Strong: a short noise burst into a feedback delay of 1/f with a lowpass
                   in the loop; pentatonic notes.
  Marimba        - FM: sine carrier + sine modulator at 4x frequency, modulation index falling fast;
                   200-1200 Hz, 0.4 s decay.
  Chiptune       - square and 25% pulse waves, quick arpeggios (3 notes in 60 ms), noise-channel
                   snare; strict 8-bit feel.
  Woodblock Kit  - bandpassed noise clicks (1.5-3 kHz, 30 ms), a pitched woodblock (triangle 800
                   Hz, 60 ms), a shaker (highpassed noise, 120 ms).
  Toy Piano      - two detuned triangles + a short high "tine" partial, quick decay; slightly out of
                   tune on purpose.
  Warm Pad       - two detuned sawtooths through a slow lowpass sweep, 0.3 s attack; for calm apps
                   and ambient beds, quiet.
  Cartoon FX     - pitch sweeps: "boing" (sine 200->600 Hz with vibrato), "pop" (noise + 900 Hz
                   blip, 40 ms), "slide whistle"; for silly and kids apps.
  Music Box      - sine + 3rd harmonic with a bright 2-3 kHz partial, long 1.2 s decay, light
                   ConvolverNode reverb from a generated noise impulse.

=== ACCESSIBILITY (required) ===
Applies to the app you build above. The nightly check scans every new app with axe-core; an app
that fails stays OFF the public Games Arcade until someone fixes it by hand. Before you save,
re-read your own CSS and markup against this list:
- CONTRAST (the commonest failure - it was 8 of 12 failing apps on 27 Oct): write the palette
  once at the top as CSS variables, and put text ONLY in these pairs - dark text (#1b1030) on any
  light or bright fill; white text only on fills at least as dark as #0977d0, #1e854a, #b0005a or
  #6b2fb3. Bright fills (green #2ec27e, orange #ff9f1c, yellow, cyan, pink) ALWAYS take dark
  text #1b1030 - white on them is about 2:1. Muted / hint / label / "meta" text on a dark panel
  is exactly #a9a3c0 (or lighter) - do not invent a darker grey or slate for "dim" text (29 Oct:
  #6f6283, #6d7899 and #6382a2 all failed) - and dark panels stay no lighter than #3a3550, where
  #a9a3c0 still reaches 4.8:1. Never fade text with opacity or an rgba alpha. A selected / "on" /
  active button or tab gets a DARK fill with white text, not a bright fill.
- EVERY CONTROL HAS A NAME: buttons have visible text or aria-label (emoji-only and icon-only
  buttons need aria-label); every <input>, <select> and <textarea> has a <label> or aria-label.
  An aria-label must CONTAIN the words and numbers shown on the button: visible "Layer 1" ->
  aria-label "Layer 1: spokes", not "Spokes layer". If the visible text is a count or a symbol that
  should not be read out, mark it aria-hidden="true" and name the button with off-screen text
  instead of aria-label (axe label-content-name-mismatch; 30 Oct: 2 of the day's first 4 apps failed it).
- SWITCHES AND TOGGLES: use a real <button aria-pressed>, or a role="switch" element that also has
  tabindex="0", aria-checked, aria-label, and flips on Space/Enter.
- KEYBOARD: everything clickable is a real <button> or <a> (not a clickable <div>/<span>).
- ROLES: only put role="list" / "listbox" / "grid" / "radiogroup" on something whose children
  have the matching roles - otherwise leave the role off.
- NO FAST FLASHING (added 10 Nov 2026; seizure risk): nothing bigger than about a tenth of the screen may
  flash (go clearly brighter, then darker) more than 3 times in any second. That covers strobes,
  lightning, blinks, hit / damage / score flashes and full-screen colour changes. A strobe runs at
  most once a beat (under 3 Hz at any tempo the app uses) or as small sparkles, never as a
  full-screen white flicker. If an effect is more intense than that, it is OFF by default, turning
  it on needs a second tap after a flashing-lights warning, and it stays off when
  matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)') matches. (10 Nov: Light Jockey's strobe flashed
  the whole screen 8-9 times a second; Phosphor Lock went to 4. flash_check.js measures this.)
- FITS A REAL PHONE (added 11 Nov 2026): never size the page, a canvas or a full-screen panel with
  "100vh" alone. On a phone, vh is measured as if the browser's address bar and toolbar weren't
  there, so a 100vh play area runs under them and its bottom buttons can't be reached. Use
  height:100% (with html,body{height:100%}) or 100dvh (write "height:100vh; height:100dvh;" if you
  want a fallback); for a canvas, size it from innerWidth / innerHeight in JS. A body that centres a
  full-height app gets min-height:100%, not 100vh. Anything added after the app (a note, a footer)
  goes INSIDE the screen that scrolls, never as a loose child of a no-scroll or display:flex body.
  (11 Nov: Particle Type and Frost Crystal Garden opened 114-180px past the bottom of an iPhone;
  phone_fit_check.js imitates the bars and fails this.)
- SAVED DATA MAY BE BLOCKED (added 19 Nov 2026): never touch localStorage or sessionStorage outside a
  try/catch - not even to READ it. A browser that blocks saved data (some privacy settings, any
  sandboxed frame) throws the moment the page reads window.localStorage, and a game that reads a
  setting at start-up with nothing to catch that stops with half its screen missing. Go through two
  small helpers and nothing else:
    const load = (k, d) => { try { const v = localStorage.getItem(k); return v == null ? d : JSON.parse(v); } catch (e) { return d; } };
    const save = (k, v) => { try { localStorage.setItem(k, JSON.stringify(v)); } catch (e) {} };
  With nothing saved, or saving refused, the app starts on its defaults and plays normally.
  (18-19 Nov: 14 live Arcade games and Human Remote, Rule Pile and Common Ground stopped this way;
  storage_block_check.js opens the page where the browser refuses storage and fails this.)
=== END ACCESSIBILITY ===
=== PLAYS RIGHT (required) ===
Applies to the app you build above. On 26-27 Nov 2026 thirteen Arcade games were played from the first
tap to the last screen, and every one of the ten made by these prompts had bugs from the same short
list - none of which a five-second smoke test can see. Before you save, re-read your own code against it:
- A DOUBLE-TAP MUST NOT PRESS THE NEXT SCREEN: when a button changes the screen, whatever button the new
  screen puts under the same finger gets the second tap of a double-tap. It skipped the hand-over to the
  next writer, showed the referee's answer, banked a round unseen, answered question 1 wrong, and wiped
  the final scores (ten games, 26-27 Nov). Put this once, as the last thing in your script:
    (function () { let t0 = 0, el = null, x0 = 0, y0 = 0;
      document.addEventListener('click', e => { const b = e.target.closest && e.target.closest('button');
        if (!b || b.closest('[data-rapid]') || (!e.detail && !e.clientX && !e.clientY)) return;   // fast-tap areas, keyboard and scripted clicks: leave alone
        const now = performance.now(), gone = el && !(el.isConnected && el.getClientRects().length);
        if (now - t0 < 400 && b !== el && gone && Math.hypot(e.clientX - x0, e.clientY - y0) < 56) { e.stopPropagation(); e.preventDefault(); return; }
        t0 = now; el = b; x0 = e.clientX; y0 = e.clientY; }, true); })();
  It only works if every tappable control is a real <button> (the KEYBOARD rule above). A control that
  is drawn on a canvas, or that acts on pointerdown / touchstart, needs its own check: ignore input for
  400 ms after the screen changes.
  FAST-TAP PLAY IS THE EXCEPTION: where tapping neighbouring buttons quickly IS the game (popping
  bubbles, whack-a-mole, drum pads, a memory grid, a letter keyboard), put data-rapid on the element
  that holds those buttons. Without it the guard swallows the second of two quick taps on targets
  less than 56 px apart when the first one disappears. Never put data-rapid on a whole screen, a menu,
  or anything holding Next / Continue / Play again / Quit.
- "TAP AGAIN TO CONFIRM" NEEDS A PAUSE: a two-tap confirm for anything that throws work away (Quit,
  Start over, Clear, New game) must ignore a second tap that comes less than 700 ms after the first.
  Without it a double-tap confirms by itself (Pepper's Fire Pup Station wiped every badge and star;
  Clue Ladder quit mid-game).
- A DELAYED STEP BELONGS TO THE SCREEN THAT STARTED IT: every setTimeout, setInterval and
  requestAnimationFrame callback that changes the game must first check that the level / round / card
  it was started for is still the current one, and return if not. Capture it when you start the timer:
    const mine = round;  setTimeout(() => { if (round !== mine) return;  ...  }, 1100);
  Home, Back, Restart and "another level" do not stop timers by themselves. (Bizzy's bee landed in the
  next garden; Maggie's Magnet Hunt gave 3 stars to a pit nobody had played; Popcorn Stand skipped a
  guest; Pepper's Home button was undone a second later.) Give "new game" a fresh state object so the
  comparison works, and reset every label a past game may have changed (a Pause button left on "Resume").
- SAVE WHAT WAS EARNED WHEN IT IS EARNED: write stars, badges and best scores to storage in the same
  step that awards them, not on the screen after. A reload or a tap on Home in between loses them.
- NAMES ARE UNIQUE: if players or teams type the same name twice, keep each name as first typed and
  renumber only the repeats, checking the new name against EVERY name in the list, not just a count:
  "Sam, Sam, Sam 2" must become Sam, Sam 3, Sam 2 - never two "Sam 2"s sharing one score. Keep scores
  in an array of players, or in Object.create(null) / a Map, never in a plain {} keyed by name.
- TIES SHARE A PLACE: in any ranked table, players on the same score get the same place number
  (1, 1, 3), and the winner line names all of them. Use place = index of the first row with that score + 1.
- ONE AND MANY: "1 point", "2 points". Never "1 points", "1 notes", "1 mangoes", "A apple".
- THE RULES SCREEN SAYS WHAT THE GAME DOES: after the game works, re-read your how-to-play text against
  the code. (It said "drag" where only tapping worked, "best of 3" where points decided, and promised a
  vote after every level.) A quiz or clue card must not contain its own answer, and its facts must be
  ones you are sure of.
- A PHONE HELD SIDEWAYS (about 844 x 390): every control must be reachable - on screen, or inside a
  panel that scrolls. A fixed-height layout that pushes the answer buttons below the fold with no
  scrolling makes the game unplayable (Maggie's Workbench, Popcorn Stand's message line).
- LONG NAMES FIT: a 16-character name of wide letters must not push a button, a buzz pad or a score
  column off a 390 px screen: give flex children min-width:0 and name text overflow-wrap:anywhere.
=== END PLAYS RIGHT ===
